### Theory

The Matrix Force Method (also known as the Flexibility Method or Method of Consistent Deformations) is one of the fundamental techniques in structural analysis used to determine the internal forces, reactions, and deformations in statically determinate and indeterminate structures by using matrix operations and linear algebra to solve systems of equations derived from the equilibrium and compatibility conditions of the structure.

Structure as a whole or any substructure must satisfy 1. 2. 3. Equilibrium of forces. Displacement compatibility. Force-displacement relation

1. Equilibrium of Forces: For any structural system, equilibrium conditions must be satisfied. This implies that the sum of forces and moments acting on any section of the structure must equal zero.

2. Compatibility of Deformations: The deformations or displacements in a structure must be compatible with the boundary conditions and connectivity of the structure. This ensures that there are no gaps or overlaps in the system.
In statically indeterminate structures, where there are more unknown forces than equilibrium equations, the compatibility of deformations is used to derive additional equations.

#### Assumptions

• It obeys Hooke’s Law

• Infinitesimally small deflections

• No change in length of member for deflections normal to the length of the member

• Follows principle of superposition

• Members are inextensible

• All other assumptions of direct stiffness approach
